# Rasphia Go-To-Market Strategy

## The Core Thesis

Rasphia wins by being the first platform where **both sides of the marketplace feel like it was built for them**. Shoppers get an AI concierge that knows their taste. Merchants get a zero-commission storefront with AI doing the selling for them. The GTM strategy is to seed the supply side hard and fast — the right merchants bring credibility, catalog depth, and word-of-mouth that acquires shoppers organically.

---

## Phase 1 — Seed with Credibility (Months 1–3)

**Goal:** 50–100 founding merchants across 4–5 categories. Quality over quantity. Every storefront should be a showcase.

### What to do

- Run the Founding Merchant Program hard. 500 slots, countdown visible, real scarcity.
- Do sales manually. DM merchants on Instagram, attend local craft markets, join seller Facebook groups and WhatsApp communities.
- Offer white-glove onboarding: help them list their first 5 products yourself if needed.
- Get at least one strong merchant per city (Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Pune) to build geo-specific social proof.

### Why this works

Early merchants are not just supply — they are evangelists. A candle maker in Pune who earns ₹15,000 more in her first month because she left Meesho will tell every seller she knows. That story travels faster than any ad.

---

## Phase 2 — Category Depth (Months 3–6)

**Goal:** 500 merchants, dominant in 2–3 categories. Enough SKU depth that shoppers reliably find what they need.

- Double down on the highest-converting categories from Phase 1.
- Launch category-specific outreach: target indie skincare Facebook groups, Etsy India sellers, Instagram boutiques.
- Introduce merchant referrals: existing merchants get priority features or store upgrades for every new merchant they bring in.
- Partner with D2C-friendly content creators to review the platform (not products — the *selling experience*).

---

## Phase 3 — Demand Flywheel (Months 6–12)

**Goal:** Shoppers arrive because of the merchants, not despite the lack of them.

- Every merchant's storefront is a distribution channel. Encourage merchants to share their Rasphia link on Instagram, WhatsApp, and in packaging inserts.
- Launch "Vibe Collections" — editorially curated bundles of products from multiple merchants around a mood or occasion. Highly shareable, drives cross-merchant discovery.
- SEO: merchant storefronts indexed by Google. Long-tail queries like "handmade soy candles Bangalore" should surface Rasphia stores.
- WhatsApp broadcast lists: merchants send their existing customers to their Rasphia storefront instead of managing orders manually.

---

## Who to Approach First

### Tier 1 — Highest Priority

These merchants have the most to gain and the lowest switching friction.

**1. Instagram-native sellers**
Selling via DMs and payment links today. They have no real storefront, handle orders manually, and lose sales to friction. Rasphia gives them a full store in 5 minutes. They already have an audience — they just need infrastructure.
- How to find them: Search Instagram for hashtags like `#handmadeIndia`, `#supportsmallbusiness`, `#IndianEtsy`, `#makersindia`
- Pitch: "Your Instagram is your catalog. We give you the checkout."

**2. Meesho/Amazon sellers paying high commissions**
They know e-commerce works. They're already selling. They're just tired of giving away 15–30% per order. They have product photos, pricing, and operational knowledge — the switch cost is nearly zero.
- How to find them: Meesho seller forums, Amazon India seller Facebook groups, YouTube comments on "how to sell on Meesho" videos
- Pitch: Show the earnings calculator. Let the numbers do the talking.

**3. Artisan / handmade product makers**
Candles, pottery, jewelry, soaps, skincare — high margin, deeply personal products that benefit most from Rasphia's persona matching. A customer matched by skin type or scent preference is far more likely to convert and far less likely to return.
- How to find them: Local craft fairs, Etsy India, `#handmadeindia` Instagram, WhatsApp craft seller communities
- Pitch: "Your products are made for someone specific. We find that someone for you."

**4. Independent skincare and beauty brands**
The persona system is tailor-made for this category. Skin type, concerns, actives, fitzpatrick scale — Rasphia can match a niacinamide serum to someone with oily, acne-prone skin better than any search bar. These brands also tend to be founder-led, move fast, and love technology.
- How to find them: ProductHunt India, LinkedIn D2C founder communities, YourStory / Inc42 features on indie beauty brands
- Pitch: "Your customer is someone with combination skin who hates fragrance. We know who that is."

---

### Tier 2 — Strong Second Wave

**5. Home decor and lifestyle brands**
High AOV, taste-driven purchases, strong gifting use case. The persona system maps directly to decor preferences (minimalist, maximalist, vintage, cozy). Works especially well for Diwali/gifting season.

**6. Fragrance and wellness**
Scent preference is deeply personal. Projection, longevity, notes — these are attributes Rasphia already models. A shopper who told Rasphia she likes woody, long-lasting scents should never see a light floral again.

**7. Fashion boutiques and independent clothing brands**
Style archetype, colorPalette, body type, occasions — the persona schema supports all of it. Particularly strong for kurti boutiques, sustainable fashion labels, and streetwear brands that sell limited runs.

---

### Tier 3 — Later, Once Platform Matures

- Electronics accessories (lower personalization leverage)
- Books and stationery (harder to differentiate on persona)
- Food / FMCG (logistics complexity, low margin)

## Channels That Will Actually Work

| Channel | Why it works for Rasphia |
|---|---|
| Instagram DMs | Where indie sellers live. Personal outreach converts. |
| WhatsApp seller groups | High-trust, peer communities. One advocate = dozens of signups. |
| YouTube (seller education) | Merchants searching "how to sell online India" are actively looking for a platform. |
| Local craft markets | Meet founders in person. Nothing beats a 5-minute demo live. |
| D2C founder Slack/Discord | Early adopters, technically curious, will share if they love it. |
| Packaging inserts | Merchants tell their customers. "Find us on Rasphia" on every box. |

---

## What to Avoid Early

- **Don't go broad too fast.** Ten brilliant storefronts beat 500 empty ones. Shoppers who visit and find weak inventory don't come back.
- **Don't chase big brands.** A Nykaa-listed brand doesn't need Rasphia. The seller who's been packaging orders herself at midnight does.
- **Don't compete on logistics.** Rasphia is a discovery and storefront platform. Let merchants handle fulfillment themselves or plug into their existing couriers. Adding logistics too early kills focus.
- **Don't skip the conversation.** The best GTM asset is a merchant who calls Rasphia "the only platform that actually gets my products." That takes a real relationship, not a signup form.

---

## The One Metric That Matters in Year 1

**Merchant NPS (Net Promoter Score)**

If merchants love it enough to recommend it, supply grows for free. Everything else — shopper acquisition, GMV, revenue — follows from a marketplace where the supply side is genuinely happy.
